# GEO / AI-search-visibility category leaders, definitions and evidence base (as of 2026-10-29)

All pages were read on 2026-10-29 unless a different read date is given. Prices are as shown on vendor pricing pages on that date; "not stated" means the page or press release did not give the figure. Vendor-reported customer counts and ARR are labelled as vendor claims.

## Key question 1: Who are the category leaders in 2026, what do they measure, charge and claim?

### Takeaway
The category is led by one venture-scale company (Profound, $1.8B valuation, >$335M raised, Sept 2026), a second tier of funded specialists (Peec AI, Scrunch, Evertune, AthenaHQ, Bluefish, AirOps, Goodie) and the incumbent SEO suites (Semrush/Adobe, Ahrefs, SE Ranking, Conductor, BrightEdge) that bolted on "AI visibility" modules. Every one of them sells the same core unit, a tracked prompt run daily across a list of answer engines, to marketing teams and agencies, at $29–$999/month self-serve and custom enterprise above that; none sells to developers or runs in a build pipeline.

### Inferences
- The unit of sale across all leaders is the daily-run prompt; price per tracked prompt ranges from roughly $0.95/mo (Peec Starter, 3 engines) to $2.68/mo (Goodie Core, 5 engines), with engines sold as add-ons (Otterly charges up to $439/mo just to add Claude). A tool that does not resell engine queries at all sits outside this pricing logic entirely.
- Every pricing page names marketing teams, agencies or enterprise marketing as the buyer; the words "developer", "CI", "pull request" and "GitHub" appear on none of the eleven pricing pages read.
- "Optimization" is arriving as hosted layers (Scrunch AXP generates an AI-friendly mirror of the site; Goodie counts "optimizations" per month; AthenaHQ sells a "content optimization agent"; Profound sells an "AI Marketer" agent). None describes changing the site's source code.
- The one fact every leader shares with RankCLI is a page-level "AI readiness" audit (Scrunch Page Audits, Rankscale 94+ checkpoints, Semrush AI Search Health). These are secondary features bundled behind the prompt-tracking paywall, not the product.

## Key question 2: How do review sites and analysts name and define the category, and who do they list as leaders?

### Takeaway
There is no settled name: G2 runs two categories ("Answer Engine Optimization (AEO)", launched March 2025, and "AI Search Visibility Optimization", added March 2026), Gartner calls them "Answer Engine Visibility Tools" (first Market Guide, 2026-03-09), a16z says "GEO" and frames "reference rate" as the metric, and Google's own May 2026 guide insists that "AEO/GEO" is "still SEO". Across all of them the definition is the same: measure how a brand appears in LLM answers, benchmark against competitors, and influence it.

### Cited Findings
- G2's AEO category launched in March 2025 with 7 products and passed 150 products in about 10 months ("over 2,000%" growth); the first AEO Grid Report shipped in G2's Winter 2026 reports (December 2025) with 9 products: Leader Profound; High Performers Otterly.AI and Scrunch AI; Contenders Semrush, BrightEdge, Conductor; Niche Quattr, GetCito, GenRank.io; later additions AirOps, Hall, Waikay, Brandi, Visby AI — [G2, 2026-01-30](https://company.g2.com/news/inside-the-2000-percent-growth-of-the-aeo-software-category-on-g2)
- G2's inclusion criteria for AEO: a product must provide (1) visibility into how and how often a brand appears in AI-generated answers, (2) protection against inaccurate brand portrayals (bias, misinformation, hallucinations), (3) transparency into why an AI tool cites one brand over another, (4) competitive benchmarking of AI share of voice — [G2, 2026-01-30](https://company.g2.com/news/inside-the-2000-percent-growth-of-the-aeo-software-category-on-g2)
- G2 buyer survey cited in the same post: about half of B2B software buyers start their search with an AI chatbot instead of Google, and 74% of those use ChatGPT; G2 says a Semrush study places G2 among the top 20 most-cited domains in LLMs — [G2, 2026-01-30](https://company.g2.com/news/inside-the-2000-percent-growth-of-the-aeo-software-category-on-g2)
- G2 added "AI Search Visibility Optimization" in March 2026 (post dated 2026-04-03), defined as tools that "help organizations ensure their brand, products, and content are accurately represented" in generative search results and that "optimize content for semantic retrieval, entity recognition, and inclusion in LLM outputs"; top three by popularity at launch: Profound, Conductor, AthenaHQ — [G2, 2026-04-03](https://company.g2.com/news/new-categories-introduced-to-g2-in-march-2026)
- Gartner published its first "Market Guide for Answer Engine Visibility Tools" on 2026-03-09 (Noam Dorros, Isoke Mitchell et al.), described as helping "enterprise marketing leaders evaluate the answer engine visibility tools that enable effective AEO" — [Evertune, 2026-03-23](https://www.evertune.ai/resources/insights-on-ai/evertune-named-a-representative-vendor-in-the-inaugural-2026-gartner-market-guide-for-answer-engine-visibility-tools); the IQRush release paraphrases the guide as "an emerging category of platforms" that help organizations understand how their brands appear in LLM-powered search such as ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity — [IQRush, 2026-05-12](https://digital-release.wboy.com/business/press-releases/cision/20260512SF57497/iqrush-named-in-the-2026-gartner-market-guide-for-answer-engine-visibility-tools)

### Inferences
- Buyers, as defined by G2's criteria and Gartner's audience ("enterprise marketing leaders"), expect four things: share-of-voice tracking, accuracy/hallucination protection, citation diagnostics and competitor benchmarking. A developer tool can only credibly claim the third (why a page is or isn't citable) and must say so.
- The two search engines that matter (Google, Bing) both define the category away: AI visibility work is SEO work. That is a usable positioning anchor for a tool that runs technical SEO plus AI readiness in one pass.
- The naming split (AEO on G2/Gartner, GEO in a16z/academia, "AI visibility" on every vendor page) means a report should use "AI visibility" as the plain-language term and reference AEO/GEO as synonyms.

## Key question 3: Which site-side factors are evidence-backed for being cited by AI answer engines?

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced site-side levers are, in order: being crawlable by the engines' search bots (OpenAI and Anthropic both document that blocking the search bot removes you from answers, and Cloudflare's defaults have changed twice), being indexed and ranking in classic search (88% of ChatGPT citations come from the general search index), freshness (cited pages are ~25% newer and citation half-life is about a month), and answer-first content with statistics, quotes and tables (Princeton GEO paper plus three 2025–26 observational studies). llms.txt has no evidence of being read by any engine and two large studies show no citation effect; structured data has one controlled test showing no measurable lift (ChatGPT +2.2%, AI Mode +2.4%, AIO −4.6%) and is best treated as hygiene; off-site brand mentions out-correlate backlinks for AI visibility but are not a site-side check.

### Cited Findings

**Structured data / schema**
- Google: "Structured data isn't required for generative AI search, and there's no special schema.org markup you need to add", but it remains worthwhile for rich-result eligibility — [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide); same statement on the older "AI features and your website" page — [Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)

### Inferences
- Ranked by evidence strength, the site-side checks that matter are: (1) search-bot access per engine (OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Googlebot/Bingbot) distinct from training-bot access, including CDN defaults; (2) indexability and classic ranking health, since ChatGPT draws 88% of citations from the search index; (3) visible, dated, recently updated content with sitemap lastmod and IndexNow; (4) answer-first structure with statistics, quotations and tables. These map onto RankCLI's robots-access, technical SEO, freshness and answer-shaped-content checks.
- llms.txt should be reported as "convention adopted by developer platforms and audited by Lighthouse, not read by any answer engine", never as a citability factor; the Lighthouse audit (file exists, H1, markdown links, no server error) is the only external standard worth matching.
- Structured data belongs in the technical-SEO half of the score (rich results, Bing's stated LLM use), not in the AI-readiness half; FAQ schema in particular has negative correlations in two datasets.
- "Brand definition" and citations on-page are supported by the Princeton results (quotes, cited sources) but off-site brand mentions are the stronger signal and are not something a site audit can fix; the report should be explicit about that boundary.
- Soft-404 detection (200 + HTML for /llms.txt or /sitemap.xml, as animerm.ai does) is a real-world failure mode the Lighthouse audit explicitly handles by flagging errors, so a presence check must verify content type and content.

### Gaps
- No engine vendor (OpenAI, Anthropic, Perplexity, Google) has published a statement that it reads llms.txt; the evidence is absence of fetches plus Google's denials. No Anthropic or OpenAI statement on structured data was found.
- No controlled test of answer-first structure exists; the Princeton paper is controlled but used gpt-3.5-turbo in 2023, and the 2025–26 studies are observational.
- Cloudflare's current crawl-to-refer ratios (Oct 2026) were not retrievable; third-party 2026 figures conflict (SEOmator gives 23,951:1 vs 2,237:1 for Anthropic in different posts), so only Cloudflare's 2025 figures are cited.
- Bing has not published an IndexNow-to-Copilot citation statistic; the "7% of new URLs clicked" figure was seen only in a headline.
- The Profound 30M-citation report was not retrievable in primary form.

## Key question 4: What do none of the leaders do that a developer tool could own?

### Takeaway
None of the eleven leaders' pricing or product pages mentions running in CI, failing a build, opening a pull request, a CLI, a GitHub Action, or framework-specific fixes; their "optimization" is hosted (Scrunch's AXP mirror, Goodie's metered "optimizations", AthenaHQ's and Profound's agents). MCP servers are now common (Profound, Otterly, Semrush, Ahrefs, SE Ranking, Rankscale, Goodie, LLM Pulse) but every one is read-mostly and gated behind a paid plan, so "agent-native" alone is not differentiation; free, local, repo-aware and write-capable is. ### Inferences
- The uncontested space is the left side of the pipeline: checks that run before deploy (on a preview URL or local build), fail a build on regressions (noindex, blocked OAI-SearchBot/Claude-SearchBot, soft-404 llms.txt), and land as pull requests in the framework's idiom. Every leader starts measuring after the site is live and sends findings to a marketer's dashboard.
- "MCP server" is table stakes by October 2026; the differentiator is an MCP/skill surface that is free, works on localhost and can write fixes, which no leader offers (Profound read-only, Ahrefs read-oriented, all paid).
- A developer tool cannot credibly claim prompt tracking, share of voice or sentiment (G2 criteria 1, 2 and 4) without reselling engine queries; it should position as the diagnostic and fix layer (criterion 3, "why a page is cited") and the regression gate, and name a tracking tool as the complement.
- Lighthouse's Agentic Browsing category is the closest thing to an external standard for developer-facing AI readiness; matching its llms.txt semantics (and extending to robots access, freshness and answer-shaped content, which Lighthouse does not check) is a defensible scope statement.
- The cheapest tiers in the category ($17–$29/mo) are still prompt-metered; "free forever CLI, pay for the hosted weekly audit" is a pricing shape no leader uses.

### Gaps
- No leader's documentation was found that explicitly says "we do not open pull requests" or "we do not run in CI"; the absence is inferred from pricing and feature pages read on 2026-10-29, not from a stated denial.
- Conductor's and BrightEdge's current product pages (Conductor "Website Monitoring", BrightEdge "Autopilot" edge changes) were not read; whether either ships code-level fixes could not be confirmed.
- Profound's own MCP tool list and API docs could not be read without sign-in.
- Lighthouse's official Chrome developer documentation for the Agentic Browsing category was not found; details come from the GitHub release and third-party write-ups, and the audit count is reported as six or seven depending on source.
