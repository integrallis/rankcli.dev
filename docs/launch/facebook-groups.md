# Facebook Groups: where RankCLI can be promoted, and how

Researched 2026-09-18. Nothing has been posted or joined yet. This file is research plus
drafts for Brian to post from his own Facebook profile.

## How to read this

**Verification flags**

- **V**: I saw it on the group's own Facebook About page (`facebook.com/groups/<id>/about`),
  fetched 2026-09-18. The group URL is the source. Member counts and "posts in the last
  month" are what Facebook showed that day.
- **S**: from a secondary source such as a listicle or press release. The source is linked.
- **U**: unverified. Facebook hides it until you join (usually the full rules or pinned posts).

Caveat: the About pages were read through an automated fetch that summarizes the page. Text
in quotes is quoted rule text. Everything else is a paraphrase. **Read the full rules and
pinned posts yourself after joining**, before the first post.

**Fit score (1–5)** combines three things: whether the audience would actually use a CLI, CI
or MCP SEO/GEO tool, how active the group is, and whether there's a legitimate path to
promotion. A 5 needs all three.

### Headline findings

1. **Open promotion is rare in the groups that matter.** I checked about 60 groups. Nine
   have a verified promotion path, and most of those paths are "get admin approval first"
   rather than a promo day. Most SEO, SaaS-founder and agency groups now say "No Promotions
   or Spam" outright.
2. **Some listicle claims are stale.** Listicles say SaaS Growth Hacks has Monday/Friday
   promo threads ([auditshq](https://www.auditshq.com/blog/saas-growth-hacks), S) or "Special
   Offer Fridays" ([sevenfigureagency](https://sevenfigureagency.com/facebook-groups-digital-marketing/), S),
   and that Marketing Solved has a "Promote Your Business Wednesday" (same source, S). Today
   both About pages say "No Promotions or Spam", and both groups are nearly dormant (2 and 7
   posts in the last month, V). The old Tekpon "SaaS Council" URL now resolves to a group
   called "TNW" that bans promotion (V).
3. **Most of the activity is in the AI-coding groups.** Vibe Coding is Life has 2,566 posts
   in the last month and AI Vibe Coding Club has 825. Many famous SEO and founder groups have
   0–10. The AI-coding audience also has the exact problem RankCLI detects: client-rendered
   apps that AI crawlers can't read (see Draft 2).
4. **Several groups auto-reject or remove AI-written posts** (Superstar SEO, Local SEO
   Strategies) or require them to be labeled (Google SEO Mastermind, The Admin Bar). The
   drafts in section 3 were written with AI help. **Rewrite them in your own words, or label
   them where a group requires it.**

## 2. Engagement playbook, developer-first and adapted to Facebook groups

### 2.1 How Facebook groups differ from Reddit and HN

- **Native posts beat links.** Several groups require links in the first comment (Claude Code
  rule 4, TNW) or ban bare links (Advanced WordPress). Admins can also set Facebook's
  **Admin Assist** to auto-decline posts that contain a link, links to specific sites, posts
  from accounts a month or less old, posts from authors with no profile picture, or posts from
  people who broke group rules in the past 28 days
  ([Social Media Examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/facebook-admin-assist-streamline-your-facebook-group-content-management/)).
  The format that survives is a text post plus one screenshot, such as terminal output or a
  results table, with the link in a comment and only where links are allowed.
- **Your profile is the funnel.** People who find a post useful click your name. In groups
  where mentioning the product counts as promotion, a profile that says "Founder, RankCLI" and
  links rankcli.dev does the work without breaking any rule.
- **Admins are gatekeepers, not obstacles.** For the approval-gated groups (A1, A2, A3, A5,
  A9), send the admin the exact post text and ask a yes/no question. It's faster and much
  safer than posting and hoping.
- **Labeling AI-written content is a real rule here.** See Headline finding 4.

### 2.2 Build these artifacts before posting anything

Each one is useful with no signup, which is what makes it postable in value-only groups.

1. **The "empty div" test**: shows a client-rendered app is invisible to AI crawlers.
   `curl -s https://app.example | grep -c '<h1'` prints `0` for most client-rendered
   Vite, Lovable or Bolt apps. It's backed by the Vercel/MERJ crawler analysis
   ([Vercel, Dec 17 2024](https://vercel.com/blog/the-rise-of-the-ai-crawler)): "none of the
   major AI crawlers currently render JavaScript" (OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, ByteDance,
   Perplexity). Gemini renders via Googlebot, and AppleBot renders too. If
   `test_repos/animerm.ai` is yours or you have the owner's OK, it's the ideal before/after
   teardown: a React + Vite app with zero SEO.
2. **The Cloudflare Sept 15 check**: the curl and log commands in Draft 1. Primary sources:
   [Cloudflare blog, Jul 1 2026](https://blog.cloudflare.com/content-independence-day-ai-options/)
   and [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/01/cloudflares-new-policy-pushes-ai-companies-to-pay-for-publishers-content/).
   The two differ on scope. Cloudflare's post says "all new domains onboarding". TechCrunch says
   new customers, new sites from existing customers, and all existing Free-plan customers.
   Re-read both before posting.
3. **A copy-pasteable MCP config** from `packages/mcp-server/README.md`: local `npx`, or the
   free hosted endpoint `https://mcp.rankcli.dev/mcp/free`, with no signup either way.
4. **A GitHub Actions YAML snippet** from the root `README.md`. It needs a free
   `RANKCLI_API_KEY`, so say so. **Before linking any Action docs, fix
   `packages/github-action/README.md`**. It still says `seo-autopilot/action@v1` and "Powered
   by SEO Autopilot", which will confuse anyone who clicks through.
5. **An aggregate teardown**: run the free checks (`seo_robots_ai`, `seo_geo_check`,
   `seo_structured_data`) across 20–30 public sites in one category the group cares about,
   such as vibe-coded showcase apps or dev-tool landing pages. Publish the aggregate table as a
   gist or blog post and screenshot it for Facebook. Report percentages, and don't single out
   individual sites.
6. **The dogfooding numbers**: `docs/launch/accounts.md` records rankcli.dev at 88/100 on
   2026-09-13, and the older launch copy says 60/100. Re-run the audit right before posting and
   use the current number.

  Post as your personal profile, not a RankCLI Page. Group culture rewards people over brands.
  That's my judgment, not a documented rule.
- Build artifacts 1–3 above, and fix the github-action README.

**Phase 1, week 1: join wave 1 (at most 3 joins a day), comments only.**
Join order: **Claude Code (A1), Vibe Coding is Life (B1), Semantic SEO & AI Search (B4),
Google SEO Mastermind (A2), Superstar SEO (A3), AI Vibe Coding Club (B2).**
- Answer membership questions honestly (see "Manual tasks").
- Read every pinned post. Update the Tier B rows in this file with what you find.
- Leave 2–3 useful comments a day in total across all groups. Good topics are robots.txt and
  AI-crawler questions, "why isn't my Lovable app indexed", schema errors, and Core Web
  Vitals. **No links, no product mentions.**

**Phase 2, week 2: first value posts and approval requests.**
- Draft 1 goes in Semantic SEO & AI Search (B4), with no link.
- A version of Draft 2 without the product mention goes in Vibe Coding is Life (B1), unless
  the pinned rules allow tool posts. In that case use Draft 4.

  Draft 3. Message the Google SEO Mastermind and Superstar SEO admins with Draft 5 and ask
  whether it's OK as written.

**Phase 3, weeks 3–4: approved posts and wave 2.**
- Publish approved posts at least 3 days apart. Stay in the thread for the first 2 hours to
  answer comments. Replies matter more than the post.
- On a Saturday, post a rewritten Draft 4 in Artificial Intelligence & Deep Learning (A4).
- Join wave 2 (at most 3 a day): SaaS Founders Club (C1), The Admin Bar (C3), Next.js
  Developers (C4), Lovable AI (C5), SEO Signals Lab (B8), and AI + SEO (A5, approval-gated).
- In SEO Signals Lab, post the Draft 2 experiment with the product name removed. In SaaS
  Founders Club, post Draft 4b.

**Phase 4, month 2 onward: steady state.**
- At most 2 value posts a week in total, at most 1 promotional post a week in total, and
  daily comments.
- Drop any group where two value posts drew no substantive comments.
- Optional and low priority: A6 on a Sunday (only after you've been a member a while), A7 on a

### 2.4 Cadence limits to avoid spam flags

Meta doesn't publish numeric thresholds. What it does publish:
- The spam policy prohibits "Posting, sharing, engaging with content or creating accounts,
  Groups, Pages, Events or other assets, either manually or automatically, at very high
  frequencies". Meta "may place restrictions on accounts that are acting at lower frequencies
  when other indicators of Spam (e.g., posting repetitive content) ... are present"
  ([Meta Transparency Center, Spam](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/spam/)).
- In 12/2023 Meta stopped *demoting* posts to groups and link posts shared at high frequency
  ([CDG changes log](https://transparency.meta.com/features/approach-to-ranking/cdgs-changes-corrections)).
  That removed the reach penalty, **not** the account-level spam restrictions above.
- Third-party estimates (from vendors of multi-group posting tools, so treat them with
  caution): a first "temporarily blocked" lasts hours to 24h, and repeats escalate to 3, 7 or
  30 days. The biggest trigger they cite is identical text across groups
  ([multiplegroupposter](https://multiplegroupposter.com/blog/blocked-from-posting-in-facebook-groups/)).
- Admins filter people who hoard groups: Detailed.com rejects members of 100+ groups, and
  Product & Growth rejects members of more than 30 (both V).

**House limits.** These are conservative choices on my part, not Meta numbers:

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| Group joins | ≤ 3 per day; ≤ 12 total in month 1 |
| Wait after joining before the first top-level post | ≥ 7 days (comments only until then) |
| Top-level posts, all groups combined | ≤ 1 per day; ≤ 3 per week in month 1 |
| Promotional (product-named) posts, all groups combined | ≤ 1 per week |
| Same URL (rankcli.dev or npm) | ≤ 1 group per day; ≤ 3 groups per week |
| Same text | Never. Rewrite the opening, the example and the ask for every group |
| Links | First comment where the rules say so; never a bare link; never a URL shortener |
| After a "temporarily blocked" notice | Stop all group posting for 7 days. Keep commenting lightly |
| Automation | None. No multi-group posters, no spintax, no Facebook "share to groups" |

## 3. Drafts to post

Plain, specific, no hype. Every draft is a starting point. **Rewrite each one in your own
words for each group.** Several groups remove AI-written posts, and identical text across
groups is the main spam trigger. Anything in brackets like `[N]` must come from a real run.
Don't post with the placeholders left in.

### Draft 1: GEO / AI-search group. Value post, no link

**For:** Semantic SEO & AI Search (B4). AI + SEO (A5) as well, but ask the admin first there.
**Type:** value post, no link, with a disclosure line.

> Cloudflare's Sept 15 default change can block Googlebot, not only GPTBot. If any of your
> sites are on Cloudflare, it's worth 5 minutes.
>
> What changed, per Cloudflare's July 1 post: on pages that show ads, the new defaults block
> "Training" and "Agent" crawlers and keep "Search" crawlers allowed. The catch is crawlers
> that do more than one job. Cloudflare says those are judged by all of their behaviors, so if
> Training is blocked, a crawler that does both search and training gets blocked too.
> Cloudflare's own example is Googlebot.
>
> Who it applies to: TechCrunch's write-up says new Cloudflare customers, new sites added by
> existing customers, and all existing Free-plan customers. Paid zones keep their existing
> config.
>
> Three checks:
>
> 1. Dashboard: look at the AI crawler / Training setting under Security for each zone. Did
>    anything change on the 15th?
> 2. robots.txt, which shows what you *think* you allow:
>    `curl -s https://example.com/robots.txt | grep -iE -A3 'gptbot|oai-searchbot|claudebot|perplexitybot|google-extended'`
> 3. Origin logs, which show what actually happened. Blocked requests never reach your origin.

## 4. Don'ts specific to Facebook groups

1. **Don't post the same text, or the same link, in several groups on the same day.** It's
   the top spam trigger. The Admin Bar and Supercharge Your Web Agency also ban cross-posting
   outright.
2. **Don't use Facebook's "share to groups" feature, multi-group posting tools, or spintax.**
   Meta's spam policy covers high-frequency posting "either manually or automatically".
3. **Don't post in a group during your first week.** Don't join more than 3 groups a day.
   Admins screen out people who are in 30+ or 100+ groups.
4. **Don't put links in the post body** where the rules say links go in comments (Claude
   Code, TNW). Never post a bare link (Advanced WordPress). Never use shortened or affiliate
   URLs (SaaS Deals With Kabir, The Admin Bar, Vue.js Developers).
5. **Don't write "DM me", "comment 'SEO' and I'll send it", or anything else that pulls
   people into private messages.** Google SEO Mastermind suspends people for it immediately,
   and several other groups ban it too. Comment-to-get posts also look like engagement bait.
6. **Don't cold-DM or friend-request people you met in a group.** Almost every group with a
   rules page bans unsolicited DMs.
7. **Don't post AI-drafted text as-is.** Superstar SEO and Local SEO Strategies remove it.
   Google SEO Mastermind and The Admin Bar require a label.
8. **Don't dress up promotion as a question** ("Has anyone tried this tool I found?").
   SaaS Founders Club bans this by name, and everyone recognizes it.
9. **Don't mention RankCLI in the no-promotion groups (Tier C)**, not even as a "disclosure".
   There, the product mention *is* the promotion. Let your profile carry it.
10. **Don't tag group owners or admins in posts** (Internet Marketing Super Friends, WP Speed
    Matters). Message them privately when asking for approval.
11. **Don't share anything from inside a group outside it**, including screenshots of other
    people's posts in a blog post or on X. Nearly every group has a "what's shared here stays
    here" rule.
12. **Don't argue if a moderator removes a post.** Ask once, privately, what would have been
    acceptable. Then follow the answer or leave the group.
13. **Don't touch link-marketplace, PBN, "group buy" or "promote anything" groups**, even
    for reach. Being seen there costs developer credibility.
